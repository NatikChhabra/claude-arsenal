# Growth funnel: Short / Reel -> long -> next long

status: beta

Three products, each built for its own app's scoring, chained so a stranger who meets one ends up bingeing the channel.
The cuts themselves are skill `shorts` and the project's edit skill; this skill is what every piece must achieve and how
they link. Score a finished package with skill `growth-gate`.

## 0. Read the channel first
Pull the real numbers (Studio, Instagram Insights, or the project's analytics tool) before deciding anything. The usual
failure pattern on a new channel (seen on one at 14 days): the opener decided everything on both short apps, low CTR
plus a weak first 30 s made YouTube stop offering the longs after the first one, and three topics in three videos meant
nothing chained. Posting time, length and the app were not the cause. Subscribers lag: fix the leading numbers below.

## 1. What each app scores, and the bar
| | YouTube Shorts | Instagram Reels | Long video |
|---|---|---|---|
| The first gate | viewed vs swiped away (~1.5-2 s; frame one IS the thumbnail) | 3-s skip rate | CTR on the thumbnail + title |
| Then | APV + replays (every loop counts as a view since 31 Mar 2025) | watch time, then **sends per reach** (Instagram's head: the top signal for non-followers) | 0:30 retention, then APV, then what they watch next |
| Bar | viewed >= 70%; APV >= 55%; the target country >= 80% of views | skip <= 30% (40% = the line the data shows); avg watch >= 50%; sends >= 1 per 100 reach | CTR >= 4%; 0:30 >= 50%; APV >= 35% |
| Next stop | **related video** (the only tap in the feed) -> its long | the **profile** (follow, bio link, the grid) | end screen + playlist + suggested |
Measured on 10 Reels of one channel: every Reel at >= 50% 3-s skip stalled at 110-183 views (7 of 7); the 3 at <= 40%
reached 814-1,337 (5-12x). Not age, posting time or length (15-29 s on both sides).
An 18,000-channel study: Shorts help small channels grow but do not by themselves lift the longs, so the long earns its
own clicks and the Short's job is to hand over a reason, not to replace it.

## 2. YouTube Shorts: the formula
1. **Stop the swipe:** frame one = the premise you can see (the subject + on-screen text), the creator's line by 0.5 s.
   Claim -> test (H2: his confident claim, then the game's or the world's answer) goes first: in one channel's data 3 of
   5 H2 openers broke out, 0 of 5 others did (n = 10, p ~ 0.08: a strong lead, not proof).
2. **Zero context, whole story:** hook -> turn (~45%) -> payoff (~70%), 15-35 s. A stranger with no topic knowledge says
   what happened and why it works. The title (<= 40 chars, first person) carries the situation the picture can't, never
   explains the joke.
3. **Loop:** the last beat flows back into frame one (the biggest winners loop).
4. **Hand-off:** the Short closes its own joke and leaves ONE bigger question the long answers, in the creator's words or
   the picture. Related video = that long, set the moment the long is public; description line 1 `full video: <link>`.
5. **Audience:** the audience's language from 0.0 s (voice + captions). A Short whose views come mostly from outside
   the target country (Studio > Geography) is a wrong-audience push: don't repeat its pattern.

## 3. Instagram Reels: the formula
1. **Hold 3 s:** voice from 0.0 s (~80% watch with sound on), motion in frame one, the claim first. Skip > 40% after
   24 h = the opener failed; the next Reel tries a different moment, not a different caption.
2. **Earn the send:** end hard on the most quotable line, the one a viewer DMs a friend. Pick moments where the real
   reaction lands on something from a viewer's own life (exams, money, a boss, a plan failing); a topic-only joke rarely
   gets sent. Never add stock local cliches on top.
3. **Earn the follow:** the profile is the next step. The grid reads as one show: the same mascot corner, covers centred
   in 3:4, the 3 best Reels pinned, the bio link to the newest long; a run of Reels from one long gives a reason to follow.
4. **Its own cut:** the same moment as the Short when it works, its own edit list when a lever differs (no loop needed, a
   harder end, a Reel-first hook). Trial Reels A/B two hooks of one moment when the first 24 h are unclear.
5. Caption per the channel's rule (short; a store link + tags is a proven minimum). Meta AI translation/dubbing OFF
   (an AI voice on the creator). The first Reel the evening of the long's day, the rest at the evening peak on days
   without a Short.

## 4. The long video: good on its own, and the hub
1. **Packaging earns the impressions:** the channel title template + 3 title/thumbnail pairs in Test & Compare. A long
   under the channel median after 7 days gets repackaged, not left alone.
2. **The first 30 s keep them:** cold open ends by 0:15, promise by 0:30, topic named by 0:30, the core loop in minute
   one. A viewer arriving from a Short sees that Short's world within 10 s (YouTube's related-video guidance: pay off the
   Short's promise at once).
3. **Re-hook at ~3:00 and ~6:00**, the payoff in the last quarter, the creator's own sign-off.
4. **Hand-off:** end screen over the last 20 s = the next video on that topic/premise if it exists, else the best
   performer by APV, + Subscribe; a card where the creator mentions an earlier video.
5. **Chain the lineup (the biggest unpulled lever):** a topic whose long beats 3x the channel median gets the NEXT long
   (Part 2, banked footage, or the same premise on a new topic), out within 2 weeks (8 of 8 measured follow-ups beat
   their channel median). A Short or Reel past 1,000 views is a signal for skill `topic-pick`, not a trigger.
6. Cadence: what the creator can sustain (1-2 longs a week is a common ceiling); every Short of a long inside its first 7
   days. Gaps over 7 days cost the next video's first push.

## 5. Playlists and the binge path (the data, not the myth)
- YouTube has never confirmed playlists as a ranking boost. What they do: autoplay the next video ("watch in playlist"),
  give the end screen and the channel page a next step, and rank in search under their title. A one-video playlist does
  none of this.
- Every long joins its TOPIC playlist, named as people search it (the game's name + the audience's language word, never
  a pun; checked across the big channels in one niche: all name playlists after the game). A saga in its order.
- A binge playlist (best-first by 0:30 retention, named for what a viewer wants) only with the creator's yes.

## 5b. Standalone saga episodes (Day N films)
Each episode is the hub of its own funnel: complete in itself, never "Part N". Its Shorts and Reels speak only in the
episode's own recorded lines; the hook carries the day or the money when it helps a stranger (both stay real); the
bigger question each Short leaves open = the episode's day question; the trailer Short is its strongest standalone
moment, never a montage. The end screen points at the next day once it exists; the saga playlist runs in day order.

## 6. The loop: measure each link, change one thing (weekly, skill `weekly-review`)
| Link | Metric (where) | Bar |
|---|---|---|
| Short stops the swipe | viewed vs swiped (Studio > Short > Engagement) | >= 70% |
| Short -> long | related-video clicks + the long's "Shorts" traffic source | baseline at the first long's day 7, then +25% a video |
| Reel holds | skip rate, avg watch (Insights) | <= 30%, >= 50% |
| Reel -> profile | sends per 100 reach, profile visits, follows, bio-link taps | sends >= 1 |
| Long earns impressions | impressions + CTR (Studio > Reach) | CTR >= 4% |
| Long keeps them | 0:30, APV (Studio > Engagement) | >= 50%, >= 35% |
| Long -> next | end-screen CTR, playlist views, views per viewer | baseline, then up each video |
Each change ships with the number it should move and the date it is read.

## 7. Live-channel audit (after every release and each weekly review)
Every public long: in its topic playlist; end screen points to a live video; Test & Compare pairs ran. Every Short:
related video = its long; the target-country share; not restricted (Studio > Content > Restrictions). Channel Home:
the featured video above the Shorts shelf (Studio > Customization > Layout), read back after a reload. Instagram: the 3
best Reels pinned, bio link = the newest long. Anything public that changes (names, pins, links) needs the creator's OK
first, unless it is part of a release he already approved.
