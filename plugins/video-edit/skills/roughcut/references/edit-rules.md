# Edit rules for a voice-led gameplay comedy cut

What happens when, in a gameplay commentary video. How each device looks and sounds (zoom sizes and budget, text, motion
feel, SFX choice, placement and caps, music levels) is in **skill design, references/edit.md**; it is not repeated here.
Evidence notes name the reference channels or the measurement behind a rule. The creator's own logged taste wins over any
rule here.

## Structure
- **The format:** cold open -> promise -> 4-6 acts -> payoff + callback + the creator's live sign-off. A calm vibe with sharp
  funny spikes, never fast and loud the whole way.
- **Cold open:** the strongest real moment (often from the middle) that PAYS OFF THE THUMBNAIL, ending by 0:15, then
  straight into the story. It may tease ONE bigger moment from later, done by the edit with the creator's own audio and
  picture, never performed or written; that payoff lands in the last third. No intro sting, no channel logo, never "hello
  guys" setup. (Top channels open cold.)
- **Promise (starts by 0:30, 10-45 s):** the creator's VO and/or live lines over quick clips (1-2 s each) of the video's own
  payoffs, never another creator's clips. It SHOWS what each sentence says: the game's title card, the screen or number
  named, then clips. By 0:30 the creator has SAID the game's name and what is happening, the picture shows what the
  thumbnail and title promised, and minute one shows the game's core loop. ~20-30 visual changes in minute 1 (measured:
  SMii7Y minute 1 = 29 cuts, SSundeePlus 26 cuts/min in 0:00-0:30).
- **What this game is, in one sentence, within 30 s of its name,** in the creator's words, over the game's logo or first
  wide; each feature named gets a 1-3 s real clip.
- **Acts on the game's own milestones,** each opened by a bridge (the creator's VO or live line: where we are + the number)
  and ending on a turn ("but..."). Chapter cards or bands only when the creator asks.
- **Something new every <= 90 s** (an upgrade, purchase, tool, twist, number); in build/upgrade games a visible step every
  ~15-25 s. **A re-hook every <= ~4 min.** A fresh beat near 3:00 and 6:00 (common retention dips).
- **The real clock is the spine:** the day count, money, or minutes out of the goal, said in bridges and shown only when it
  changes, in the creator's words or the game's. A time card only with a real number ("2 hours later" without one is out).
- **Pacing curve:** hot sections separated by 20-40 s breathers that play straight; the second-best moment at ~70% of
  runtime; the rush only rises or holds act to act; the last minute is a high (peak-end).
- **Talk is the calm glue:** talk covers >= 70% of the runtime; a voiceless stretch over 5 s only when the game is the show
  (<= 1 per 3 min). Thinking out loud stays ("let's try this", "I think..."): it is the setup the payoff needs.
- **Length:** keep what earns its place. If only 8 funny minutes exist, ship 8. Never pad for the algorithm.
- **Intro different every video** (an experiment built from this video's moments; log the structure and its 30 s
  retention so a winner can become the default). **Outro = the creator's own live sign-off** from the recording, right
  after the last real moment, with no produced outro, recap montage or voice-sting stack after it.
- **End on the goal:** payoff + callback, then the sign-off within ~30 s; no wind-down.
- **Post-sign-off stinger (try once):** one real leftover funny moment under the end screen, never a recap.

## Cuts
- **Every cut moves the story:** after minute 2 a cut skips to the next step (a task done, a purchase, the reaction to it)
  or removes dead air; a building situation plays whole. No fixed cut rate (references range 4.6-14.8 cuts/min).
- **Keep the setup with the punchline:** the 5-15 s that make it land (what was tried, the number before). A piece ends
  where the creator's bit ends + a beat, never mid-riff.
- **Deadpan hold on ~1 in 3 punchlines,** only where the deadpan is real; it counts as a device, not dead air.
- **A jump that skips to the next step needs no transition:** the cut is the step. A jump to another place or time does
  (a dissolve, a card with a real number, or the time skip said in the creator's VO).
- Cut mechanics (reaction timing, hold lengths, dead air, J-cuts, fast-forward, slow-mo, cadence, bosses): skill design,
  references/edit.md §1.2-1.4.
- **Escalating bits run** while they keep escalating; cut hard on the peak.

## Comedy engines (how the edit makes a moment funny)
| Engine | How |
|---|---|
| Make the expectation explicit | the plan is on screen before it breaks: the brag captioned, the UI's promise (stars, timer) punched in |
| Contrast | calm vs chaos: a flat caption on a disaster; a dramatic look on something trivial; the sound dropping out on the violation |
| Rule of three | show it twice, break it the third time; a repeated fail gets shorter each round, the last lands fastest; never a supercut recap |
| Callbacks | keep every instance of a recurring phrase; the payoff replays the earlier moment for 1-2 s; the setup is on screen before every callback |
| Reaction first | the reaction frame BEFORE the cause, then the cause wide (a hard-cut reorder; no "10 seconds earlier" card unless the number is real) |
| Promise vs result | the confident line -> hard cut to the broken result FULL-SCREEN -> the flat line; no box, no label |
| Psych reversal | a sensible-sounding sentence, the cut lands the opposite on its last word |
| Silence / music drop (try once) | on a "wait, what" beat the bed drops out (the game sound stays), then back |
| Self-deprecation | the creator's own low-expectation lines kept so small wins feel big; never written by the editor |
| Escalation runs | the dumbest real consequence of a tiny action gets the full treatment |
| Specificity | jokes about what is really on screen (read GAME.md first) |
| The edit performs the line | talk about speed and the cut gets fast; "slow down" and the shot breathes |
| Rewind gag | "wait, rewind": a short reversed piece, then the moment replayed slower with a punch-in; max ~1 a video |
| Running gags | the creator's own, built from what they say; never editor-invented UI gags (counters, meters) |
| One rewind-worthy moment per act | a fail, an absurd moment or a milestone reward given the full treatment (setup, hit, reaction) |
| VO sets up, live pays off | a VO line states what is about to happen and ENDS on the frame the live reaction starts; never describes what the clip then shows |

## On-screen words
- Only the creator's exact words (a key-phrase slam at the peak of a line, <= 5 words) or real game/UI words (labels,
  prices, counters). Mark each with its source: the line around it, `game`, or a callback time.
- Never: editor labels ("ATTEMPT #7"), editor irony ("(he was not fine)"), "LESSON COMPLETE", BEFORE/AFTER tags, kinetic
  WHAT/HOW/WHY, "?" stickers, a card that only echoes the line just said.
- No emoji pops, meters, framed memes or sticker reactions as a template (the full list of machine-made tells: skill
  design, references/edit.md §8); memes are rare and picked by the creator's meaning.
- Readable chaos: stack what each beat needs, a different mix each time, never across the punchline word, never a device
  after every line (warn past 40% of lines ending in an added device).

## Inserts
- Real only: the game's own frames, the brand character's approved poses, licence-clean (CC0) images. An AI-made insert
  only if a blind reader cannot tell it is AI.
- Never film, TV, anime or news clips, songs, other creators' clips or audio, meme sounds from songs or films.

## Safety
- No slurs, nothing punching down (caste, religion, region, disability, gender), nothing sexual about anyone's family.
  Swearing is fine in audio; keep the first minute lighter.
- **No-go words** (slurs, anyone's real name, school or city, a friend's personal info): a bleep span in SOURCE seconds
  from the word times, so the audio is muted + toned and the caption shows `****`. List every bleep in the report.
- **Swears the ASR spelled clean:** a second model or a swear list flags them; bleep each one the cut keeps.
- **On-screen privacy:** other players' gamertags, kill-feed names, chat, Discord/Steam/OS popups, a friend's name, a
  phone notification -> a blur box (fractions of the source frame) on that segment, split to time it. Grab a frame to
  place it. List every box in the report.
- **The creator's identity (faceless channels):** every span the privacy scan printed (skill `after-recording` step 3) gets
  its box on every piece that shows it; check each popup by eye.
- **Monetization:** commentary >= 35% of runtime, no 45 s silent stretch, <= 1.5 unbleeped strong swears a minute.

## Recorded in blocks
Transcribe each block, then join blocks losslessly (same recorder profile; a different resolution needs a re-encode) into
one source with one merged transcript that marks where each part starts.

## Second angle (VR or a POV capture)
When the recorder writes a second file (the player's eyes vs a spectator view), use the POV where the joke is in the
player's hands or face (a held item, a jam, reading a note) and the spectator view where the body, world or a fall is the
joke. Sound always comes from the main recording. Check one sharp moment (a shot, a flash) in both before the cut ships.
