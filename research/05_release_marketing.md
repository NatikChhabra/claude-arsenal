# Release + marketing research: indie guitarist, AI-assisted vocals, GTA V RP characters

Researched 2 Nov 2026. Tags: **CONFIRMED** = read on the primary page at the URL. **CONFIRMED (2nd)** = read on a
credible secondary page that quotes the primary, because the primary would not load (proxy/robots). **INFERRED** = my
reading or a third-party number with no primary source. Page dates are the page's own date where it gives one.

---

## 0. The five findings that change the plan

1. **The "AI Persona" trap (Spotify, live since mid-Sep 2026).** Spotify badges any artist whose *visual identity* is
   an AI-generated, photorealistic, human-like person. Their music is then left out of **all** editorial and algorithmic
   recommendations, so it gets no Release Radar reach, no Discover Weekly and no Radio. It is also blocked from Discovery
   Mode. An RP character "artist" shown with photoreal AI portraits is at real risk of this. Use real in-game GTA screenshots
   or clearly stylised art for the profile and covers, and keep a real human (the owner) visible as the artist identity.
   The badge is about identity, not about how the music was made (#11-#14).
2. **The distributor decides whether AI-assisted vocals can ship at all.** CD Baby refuses any AI content, even partial.
   TuneCore takes it only if every GenAI tool was trained on fully licensed data. Amuse and RouteNote accept it, but AI
   releases do **not** go to YouTube Content ID (Amuse also leaves out Meta and Qobuz; RouteNote leaves out Amazon and the
   Korean stores). DistroKid accepts it if you own 100% of the rights, there is no impersonation, and you tick the AI box
   at upload. That last rule is CONFIRMED (2nd): DistroKid's own page would not load (#20-#27).
3. **Pitch 7+ days before release, and deliver 7+ days before.** Without that the song misses the first-week Release
   Radar slot. Spotify's 2024 guide now says **two weeks** for pitching. Countdown Pages (pre-save) are **albums/EPs only,
   not singles**, and need 5,000 monthly active listeners. Marquee and Showcase cost money (USD 100 minimum, 250 for
   Marquee booked through a rep) and need 1,000 streams in 28 days. They are not available to teams based in India.
   Discovery Mode needs 25,000 monthly listeners and takes a 30% commission (#1-#10).
4. **Disclosure is required on YouTube, Instagram and TikTok when AI music or realistic AI audio is the point.**
   YouTube: "AI-generated music" and "music that's the main focus" must be disclosed, but "cloning one's own voice"
   for voiceovers/dubs does not need it. Instagram: "realistic-sounding audio" must be labelled. TikTok: "realistic
   images, audio or video" must be labelled. Disclosure alone does not limit reach on YouTube (#33-#37).
5. **RP servers: the hard limits come from the platform, not just the server.** The Cfx.re Creator PLA (10 Sept 2026)
   bans third-party "promotions, advertisements, endorsements, or sponsorships" inside Creator Content (3.1(7)), and
   bans uploading "any musical work ... subject to license by a third party" (2.4). In-character songs need to be
   self-owned originals, never sponsored. Server rules commonly ban cross-server promotion and metagaming, and some
   allow only "DMCA-safe music inside the city" (#45-#52).

**Spotify AI policy 2025-2026**
- 25 Sep 2025: impersonation policy (vocal impersonation only with the impersonated artist's authorisation), a spam filter
  (mass uploads, duplicates, SEO tricks, tracks just over 30 s), and an AI disclosure standard through DDEX. Spotify
  says it "won't force tracks into a false binary" and disclosure won't down-rank (#11, CONFIRMED (2nd) via MBW).
- AI credits (beta): mark specific roles (lyrics, vocals, instrumental performances, production) as AI-generated
  through a distributor that supports it. Shown in song credits and Now Playing, not on the profile (#12).
- AI Persona badge (announced 11 Aug 2026, rolled out mid-Sep 2026): "an AI-generated photorealistic human-like
  identity". "Music from AI Personas is not included in any editorial or algorithmic recommendations" (#13, #14).

## 3. Release strategy evidence

- About 75% of a release's first-year streams come **after** the first 28 days (Spotify Fan Study; tracks released Feb
  2024) (#39). A song is not "done" after week 1, so plan teasers and posts for at least 4 weeks after release.
- A new release lifts catalogue streams about 15-20% (Nov 2020 data). Profile visits rise 77% on release day (#38).
  This argues for a steady single cadence.
- Six months after saving a track, listeners stream it about 3x as much as before saving (#38). Saves are the metric
  to push.
- Fans who watch a music video stream that artist 57% more over the next 3 weeks (#40).
- Scale of competition: 106,000 new tracks a day in 2025; 88% of tracks got under 1,000 streams in 2025 (Luminate via
  MBW) (#41).
- **Waterfall releases:** no primary Spotify, Luminate or Chartmetric study found. The closest primary evidence is
  "a single before the album doubles album pre-saves" (#38). Mark waterfall as INFERRED practice.
- **Teaser length:** primary evidence points to **7-14 days**: Countdown 7+ days doubles pre-saves (#38); TikTok's
  New Release tag runs from 14 days before to 30 days after (#30); YouTube says tease, then make Shorts across pre-,
  release-day and post-release (#31).

## 4. Short-form for music

**TikTok**
- Artist Account: open to any artist who has released music. The Artist Tag needs 4 songs uploaded. The Music tab fills
  itself. The New Release tag shows from 14 days before to 30 days after release (#30, Nov 2023).
- TikTok for Artists (global since 3 Jun 2025): daily analytics per song/post/follower, plus a Pre-Release pre-save for
  **albums** (Spotify and Apple Music only). Needs a certified Artist Account (#29).
- Commercial Music Library: about 1M tracks "free for businesses". Business accounts see only Commercial Sounds; personal
  accounts see all sounds (#28). **INFERRED:** run the artist page as a personal/creator account so your own released
  track (delivered via distributor) is pickable, and post with that official sound rather than a re-upload of the audio,
  so creations stack on one sound page.
- TikTok + Luminate (Feb 2025): users "74% more likely to discover and share new music"; Add to Music App "more than
  one billion track saves"; 84% of Billboard Global 200 entrants went viral on TikTok first (#32).

**YouTube Shorts**
- YouTube's artist guide: "always use the official song from the audio picker"; post weekly, then adjust. In Jan 2024
  Shorts with an artist's music tripled average unique viewers. Artists posting weekly got over 60% of new subscribers
  from Shorts (#31).
- Remix: "Use this sound", Green Screen, Cut (1-5 s). Up to 90 s of audio from a music video, some capped at 30 s.
  Shorts are opted in to remixing by default (#42).
- Shorts over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim are **blocked**. The Shorts audio library may allow up to 90 s
  of a track (#43). Your own track only shows in the picker once it is distributed with a Shorts-agreement partner and
  has a playable art track (#43).

**Instagram Reels**
- Originality: duplicates are not recommended (the original replaces the copy); no visible watermarks for
  recommendation eligibility (#44). **INFERRED:** never repost a TikTok export with its watermark; export clean.
- Trial Reels: shown to non-followers first; metrics after 24 h; optional auto-share if it performs (Dec 2024; CONFIRMED (2nd) via Tubefilter) (#53).
- AI label required for "realistic-sounding audio" (#36).

**Hook selection (which 15-30 s):** no primary platform source gives a rule. YouTube advises "many different Shorts
for one song" (#31), so treat hook choice as a test: cut 3-4 different 15-30 s sections (chorus, the strongest guitar
riff, the line that matches the RP story beat) and let Trial Reels and Shorts numbers pick (INFERRED).

## 5. YouTube

- **Official Artist Channel:** you need a single artist channel focused on music, at least 1 official release
  delivered by a distributor or label, and compliance with policies. You also need one of: a Partner Manager, YPP, a
  label network, or a distributor listed in the YouTube Services Directory. Request it via the distributor. A Community
  Guidelines strike suspends OAC status (#33).
- **Art Track** = the auto-made audio-plus-cover video from the distributor's DDEX feed. An official music video exists
  only when the artist makes one (#34). **OMV vs lyric video vs visualiser:** YouTube's Song Rollup pools official music
  videos, art tracks, lyric videos and live versions into one song stat (#35). Every format you make feeds the same
  song number. No YouTube-published performance ranking of the formats was found.
- **AI disclosure:** required for realistic AI content, including "Creates music that's the main focus of the video"
  and "AI generated music". Not required for "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs", unrealistic or
  animated content, or minor edits. A player label may appear for AI music. Disclosure alone does not limit audience or
  monetisation. Repeated non-disclosure can mean removal or YPP suspension (#37). **INFERRED:** AI-assisted *singing*
  that is the song's lead vocal should be disclosed.
- Titles and thumbnails (YouTube Help): the title must match the video; front-load key terms; limit ALL CAPS and emoji;
  readable thumbnail text, simple composition; judge CTR on Home/Suggested in the first 24 h (#54).

## 6. Copywriting rules from credible sources (checkable)

From Spotify's playlist editors (Spotify for Artists blog, 23 Jul 2020) (#55):
1. Give "the who, what, why, when, where, and how": who worked on it, why and when it was made, where it was recorded.
2. Include the story angle and **marketing plans** (music video, release schedule, promos).
3. Fill mood and genre fields accurately: "incredibly helpful".
4. Follower counts and blog/radio write-ups don't factor in. Never leave the note blank. Paid playlist offers break the rules.

Practitioner rules (Jesse Cannon, Music Marketing Trends, 10 Feb 2026; INFERRED for the 500-character limit, which is
not on a Spotify page I read) (#56): 500 characters; lead with the one exciting thing; use scene micro-genre words,
never "this belongs on a major playlist"; list concrete promo.

From YouTube Help (#54): the title must match the content; key terms first; caps/emoji sparingly.

From Spotify Canvas guidelines (#6): leave out the song and artist name (already shown).

From Instagram (#44): no visible watermarks.

**Arsenal check list (INFERRED, combining the above with the repo's no-slop rule):** each pitch or caption names one
concrete, checkable fact (RP character, the in-city event, where it was recorded, who played the guitar), uses no hype
adjective with no fact behind it, and contains no follower-count bragging.

## 7. Community / GTA RP

- **Cfx.re Creator Platform License Agreement (PDF dated 10 Sept 2026; fivem.net/terms redirects there)** (#45-#47):
  - 3.1(7): no Creator Content with "third-party promotions, advertisements, endorsements, or sponsorships", in game
    or as Marketplace Content.
  - 3.1(5): no server run "in an ongoing commercial association" with a third-party brand.
  - 2.4: no creating/uploading "any musical work or sample thereof that is subject to license by a third party";
    no right to perform or remix Rockstar's licensed music; no altering game voice recordings.
  - 2.3: nothing may suggest the server is approved, sponsored or endorsed by Rockstar; a disclaimer is required.
- Rockstar's RP page only points to its ToS, the PLA and the Mod Guidelines (#48).
- Example server rules (public pages):
  - BigDaddy: "Advertising of any other businesses, communities, or servers will not be tolerated." You *can*
    advertise your Twitch/YouTube in your name format. Metagaming (OOC info used IC) is banned, including watching
    streams for advantage (#49).
  - True North (#25): "strictly prohibited from advertising or promoting other FiveM servers, services, or
    communities" (#50).
  - Rainy Day RP: "Play only DMCA-safe music inside the city"; cross-server promotions prohibited (#51).
  - Fat Duck Gaming: OOC information used IC is "strictly prohibited" (#52).
  - NoPixel's rule page was not found publicly in this pass. Do not cite NoPixel rules without reading them.
- Precedent: NoPixel characters released in-character music videos, e.g. Zerkaa (Tommy T) and SparkyKNE (Sparky Kane),
  "Here To Stay", Dec 2022, made entirely in-server (#57); xQc and MaximusBlack "Bish Don't Take My Phone" (#58).
- **INFERRED playbook (check each server's own rules first):** perform the song IC (in-city radio or an event) only if
  the server allows custom audio and it is the owner's own original; keep streaming links in the OOC name/bio where
  allowed, never as an IC ad; never name other servers; no real-brand sponsorship inside the city (PLA 3.1(7)).
  Promotion of the real release happens OOC (YouTube, Instagram, Discord), with the RP story as the hook.

## 8. Measurement (day 1 / 7 / 28)

- **Spotify:** Release engagement = the % of monthly active listeners (on the day before release) who streamed the new
  release in its first 28 days. It updates daily, in UTC (#59). Listeners = unique listeners in the period (#60). Real-time
  streams cover the first 7 days; check stats about a week after release (#8). Save rate is **not** a Spotify metric:
  compute it as saves / listeners per song (#61, INFERRED).
- **Save-rate "good" numbers:** no Spotify, Luminate or Chartmetric benchmark was found. The only published tiers are a
  vendor's (Dynamoi: 20-25% good, 25%+ excellent; its own campaign data, method not shown) (#61, INFERRED). Use the
  artist's own trend: compare each release's day-28 save rate with the last.
- **YouTube:** OAC Analytics for Artists: Total Reach, Song Rollup, real-time 60 min/48 h, "Fastest Moving Songs in
  Shorts" by Shorts creations (#35). YouTube's artist guide names Creations, Subscribers from Shorts and Song Views from
  Shorts as first metrics (#31). Read CTR on Home/Suggested in the first 24 h (#54).
- **TikTok for Artists:** views, posts and creator engagements per track; completion rates per post (#29).
- **Suggested read-out (INFERRED):** day 1 = real-time streams, YouTube 48 h views, CTR; day 7 = Release Radar
  listeners, saves/listeners, Shorts creations, TikTok creations; day 28 = Release engagement %, save rate,
  streams/listener, playlist adds, Subscribers from Shorts.
